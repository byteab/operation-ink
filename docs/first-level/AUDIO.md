# Audio provenance and behavior

`src/game/audio.ts` plays existing samples from `public/sounds/` with original procedural Web Audio synthesis when a sample is missing or has not decoded. Sample provenance is in `public/sounds/CREDITS.md`: CC0 Kenney/OpenGameArt effects and original guard sentences rendered offline by `scripts/make-voices.sh` using macOS speech. Runtime speech synthesis is not used. Gameplay polish introduces no downloaded recordings.

Pistol, automatic and sniper reports use the authoritative weapon shot events. Sniper reports use slower, heavier versions of the existing CC0 rifle samples. Flesh impacts receive `SoundEvent.zone`: head is sharper, torso lower, arms lighter and legs intermediate. Surface hits and deaths have separate sample sets. Reload start/completion, switch, pickup and empty trigger use different short synthesized mechanical envelopes. Missing files never break gameplay and retain procedural cues.

Ladder sounds are alternating-pitch, nonrepeating sample selections emitted by actual climbing motion. The runtime controls rung cadence; the audio layer rejects duplicate events within 130 ms. There is no ladder loop. Spatial effects use HRTF panning and inverse-distance falloff, with explicit event-radius culling and a listener that follows the actual camera. Hearing for AI is simulation data and remains independent of volume or mute.

Callouts use four offline speaker sets for spot/contact/lost/search/hurt/down/reload/flank/retreat/clear. Only one spoken line may play at once, including urgent lines. There is a 350 ms gap after sampled lines. Speaker cooldowns are 3.8 seconds normally or 1.6 seconds for urgent lines; repeated speaker/intent pairs wait 8 or 4 seconds respectively. Busy lines are discarded rather than queued into stale combat contexts. When samples are unavailable the short positional cue has the same cooldown policy. Directional captions carry gameplay information independently.

A quiet filtered wind bed and original 16-second D-minor ambient phrase run during active play. Music uses soft sine layers at low gain (0.028 before the master volume); weapon reports, player damage and callouts duck it to 0.008, restoring it over 1.5 seconds. Both are bounded looping sources, with envelopes at the phrase boundary. This is synthesized atmospheric music, not a licensed game soundtrack.

Begin/Resume or the canvas unlocks browser audio. Pause/death stops and disconnects all sources and suspends the context. Resume starts one ambience/music pair. Reset clears active effects and cooldowns; the next active update restarts the bed. Mute and volume affect the master output. Disposal aborts pending fetches, ignores late decoded results, releases buffers and nodes, and closes the context; a disposed instance cannot unlock again. Effect concurrency is capped at 80 including the two background loops.

Run `node scripts/check-player.mjs scripts/polish-audio-checks.ts` for source lifecycle, sample/fallback, voice timing, hit-region, sniper, ladder and late-load checks using a fake AudioContext. `MissionAudio.diagnostics` exposes source/loop state and callout counters for runtime verification. These checks verify behavior and cleanup, not subjective mix quality. Physical-speaker/headphone listening remains a human review item.
