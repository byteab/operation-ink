# Surveillance and response

The existing enemy director supplies real collision-based vision, path planning, investigation, bounded searching, combat, and return-to-post behavior. The rescue mission adds camera observations and finite barracks activation through that director; it does not run a second enemy state machine.

Each mounted camera sweeps slowly around its authored facing. Its small indicator is green while active and dark when the security computer disables the network. The indicator changes its own material only and emits no scene light. Detection requires the player's eye to remain within the 28-degree half-angle cone and have unobstructed geometric line of sight for 0.75 seconds continuously. Cover clears accumulated detection. Disabled cameras stop both sweeping and sensing.

An alarm reports the observed ground position once. Outdoor guards investigate that position, and two existing barracks soldiers become active at their authored interior positions. Responders first walk through the authored north barracks exit, then investigate the report; this avoids congestion at the south doorway shared with the jeep route. Two more respond after 14 seconds only if the alarm remains active. Four is the mission-wide reserve cap, including repeat alarms; there is no spawning or replacement of dead soldiers. Alarm investigations allow twenty seconds per travel leg before falling back to finite local search. Actual subsequent guard vision and sounds drive combat normally. A camera report alone never grants visual confirmation or tracks the player through cover.

Reserve soldiers use the existing controlled-activation system: four actors are allocated at mission load but hidden while dormant. Ordinary room guards remain visible. Alarm dispatch reveals the designated actors inside the crew house and makes them walk out through its doors; it never creates soldiers behind the player or restores a killed responder.

The existing horn sound repeats every seven seconds while the alarm is active. Silencing stops future horn pulses and cancels any pending second wave. Responders without direct combat contact search briefly using existing local search points, then resume patrols or return to their barracks posts. Soldiers with visual contact keep fighting. The HUD's recently silenced state clears after twelve seconds. Active cameras can trigger another alarm after another full detection dwell, so disabling cameras before silencing offers a lasting advantage.

`SecuritySystem` owns alarm timers; the mission owns the authoritative alarm/camera fields and reserve count. `sync()` immediately applies panel state and `reset()` clears transient detection and visual caches. Enemy snapshots include alarm response ownership. Runtime restores mission and enemy snapshots together, then synchronizes camera visuals.

Run focused regression checks with `node scripts/check-player.mjs scripts/security-checks.ts`. They exercise geometric occlusion and interrupted dwell, timed activation, no hidden-position tracking, disabled indicators/sweeps, cancellation before the second wave, bounded search recovery, repeat-alarm limits, legitimate combat after silencing, and snapshot/reset behavior. Actual-level checks verify every mounted camera has usable geometric sightlines and all four staged responders physically exit the barracks.
